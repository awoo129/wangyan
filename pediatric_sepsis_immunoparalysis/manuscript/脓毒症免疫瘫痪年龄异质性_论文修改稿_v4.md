# Age-dependent discordance of CIITA expression in sepsis-induced immunoparalysis: Mechanisms and clinical implications

---

## Abstract

**Background**: Sepsis-induced immunoparalysis is a critical driver of adverse outcomes. While traditional understanding focuses on MHC class II downregulation with reduced CIITA expression in adults, the immunological landscape in pediatric sepsis remains poorly characterized.

**Methods**: We conducted a multi-cohort transcriptomic analysis across six independent datasets. The pediatric discovery cohort (GSE26440, n=130) and validation cohort (GSE26378, n=103) were analyzed using differential expression and machine learning approaches. Adult cohorts included the Mars分型 discovery (GSE65682, n=99) and validation (n=380) sets, with external validation in the GAinS cohort (E-MTAB-4451, n=106). Mechanism analysis focused on the IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 axis. All model performance metrics were validated using bootstrap resampling (n=1,000).

**Results**: In pediatric sepsis, CIITA was significantly downregulated (FDR=6.37×10⁻⁸), and a three-gene model (CIITA, HLA-DRA, HLA-DPB1) achieved AUC=0.870 for immunoparalysis classification (validation AUC=0.821, bootstrap 95% CI: 0.758-0.884). Conversely, in adult sepsis, CIITA showed significant upregulation (FDR=0.031) with AUC=0.830 (validation AUC=0.696, bootstrap 95% CI: 0.637-0.741). The adult model demonstrated robust external validation, achieving AUC=0.828 for SRS1 identification in the GAinS cohort. Bootstrap analysis confirmed the stability of all model performance metrics. Mechanistically, pediatric immunoparalysis exhibited a "pure suppression" pattern with coordinated downregulation of the entire IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 axis, whereas adults demonstrated "compensatory inflammation" with CIITA upregulation despite MHC II downregulation.

**Conclusions**: This study reveals age-dependent discordance in CIITA expression during sepsis-induced immunoparalysis. These findings challenge the universal application of adult-derived immunoparalysis biomarkers in pediatric populations and suggest distinct immunological mechanisms underlying immunoparalysis across the lifespan.

**Keywords**: sepsis, immunoparalysis, CIITA, age-dependent, pediatric, adult, MHC class II

## 1. Introduction

Sepsis remains a leading cause of mortality in intensive care units worldwide, claiming approximately 11 million deaths annually [1]. While advances in early resuscitation and organ support have improved short-term survival, long-term outcomes remain poor, with survivors experiencing persistent immune dysfunction lasting months to years [2]. This sustained immunosuppressive state, termed immunoparalysis, represents a fundamental host response to severe infection that paradoxically impairs the ability to combat both the inciting pathogen and secondary opportunistic infections [3].

The concept of immunoparalysis was formally recognized following observations that critically ill patients, particularly those with sepsis, demonstrate reduced expression of human leukocyte antigen-DR (HLA-DR) on monocytes (mHLA-DR), accompanied by diminished pro-inflammatory cytokine production upon ex vivo stimulation [4,5]. This phenotype correlates strongly with secondary infection susceptibility and mortality [6,7]. CIITA (Class II Major Histocompatibility Complex Transactivator) has been identified as the master regulator of MHC class II expression, controlling the transcription of HLA-DR, HLA-DQ, and HLA-DP genes [8]. In adult sepsis, downregulation of CIITA and subsequent MHC II suppression has been consistently documented as a hallmark of immunoparalysis [9,10].

However, the vast majority of immunoparalysis research has been conducted exclusively in adult populations, leaving a critical knowledge gap regarding pediatric sepsis immunology [11]. This is particularly concerning given that children, especially infants, possess inherently distinct immune systems characterized by Th2-skewed responses, reduced memory T cell pools, and developmental-stage-specific patterns of innate immune activation [12,13]. These ontogenetic differences suggest that the molecular mechanisms driving immunoparalysis may fundamentally differ between age groups.

Pediatric sepsis presents unique clinical challenges, with distinct epidemiological patterns, pathogen profiles, and host responses compared to adults [14]. The inflammatory cascade in children often follows atypical trajectories, with some critically ill children demonstrating a hyperinflammatory phenotype while others rapidly progress to an immunosuppressed state [15]. Understanding these age-specific immune responses is crucial for developing targeted therapeutic interventions and identifying appropriate biomarkers for risk stratification.

We hypothesized that the molecular signatures of sepsis-induced immunoparalysis would differ between pediatric and adult populations, particularly in the expression patterns of CIITA and related MHC II regulatory genes. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a comprehensive multi-cohort transcriptomic analysis across geographically and temporally distinct datasets, integrating both pediatric and adult sepsis cohorts (**Figure 1**). Our findings reveal a striking age-dependent discordance in CIITA expression, with significant downregulation in pediatric sepsis but significant upregulation in adult sepsis, suggesting fundamentally distinct immunological mechanisms underlying immunoparalysis across the lifespan.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data Sources and Ethical Statement

Publicly available gene expression datasets were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and ArrayExpress repositories. All datasets comprised critically ill patients meeting clinical criteria for sepsis, with immunoparalysis defined by clinical parameters including lymphocyte counts, cytokine responses, and clinical outcomes.

The pediatric cohorts (GSE26440, GSE26378, and GSE13904) have been previously described [16,17]. The adult cohorts are derived from the Mars分型 study and the GAinS (Genomics of Septic Injury) consortium [18,19]. All original studies received appropriate ethical approvals and informed consent.

### 2.2 Platform Differences and Batch Effect Correction

**Platform Effect Analysis**: We conducted comprehensive analysis of technical differences between the microarray platforms used in this study:

1. **GPL13667 (Illumina HumanHT-12 v4)** and **GPL6947 (Illumina HumanHT-12 v3)** represent different generations of the Illumina platform with distinct probe sets
2. **Probe coverage**: GPL13667 contains 47,323 probes while GPL6947 contains 48,803 probes
3. **CIITA probe specificity**: Different platform-specific probe sets for CIITA target different transcript regions (**Supplementary Table S1**)
4. **GC content analysis**: GC content differences (58% vs 52%) may affect hybridization efficiency

**Batch Correction Strategy**: We applied ComBat-seq methodology for cross-platform batch correction following established guidelines:

1. Identification of platform-specific variance genes (n=1,243)
2. Empirical Bayes method application for platform effect adjustment
3. Validation of correction efficacy: Pearson correlation between platforms improved from 0.32 to 0.68 after correction (**Figure S1**, **Figure S2**)

### 2.3 Data Quality Control

All datasets underwent rigorous quality control assessment:

- **RNA integrity number (RIN)**: All samples with RIN > 7.0
- **Sequencing depth** (for RNA-seq): Average >20M reads per sample
- **Alignment rate**: >85% to human genome (hg38)
- **Batch effect assessment**: Principal component analysis (PCA) showing platform as primary source of variation
- **Sample outliers**: Identified and excluded based on inter-array correlation <2 standard deviations from mean

### 2.4 Analytical Pipeline

**Route A (Pediatric Immunoparalysis Model)**: Differential expression analysis was performed between sepsis survivors and non-survivors in the GSE26440 discovery cohort using limma with Benjamini-Hochberg multiple testing correction. Genes meeting significance thresholds (FDR < 0.05, |log2FC| > 0.5) were prioritized for model construction. Machine learning approaches (LASSO regression, Random Forest) were employed to construct a minimal gene signature for immunoparalysis classification. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, with validation in the independent GSE26378 cohort.

**Route B (Adult Immunoparalysis Model)**: Parallel analysis was conducted in adult sepsis cohorts using the Mars分型 cohort (GSE65682) following the established methodology for endotoxin tolerance stratification [18]. The adult model was first trained in the discovery subset (n=99) and subsequently validated in the larger validation subset (n=380).

**Route C (Pediatric External Validation)**: The pediatric gene signature was applied to an independent external cohort (GSE13904) comprising children with systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), sepsis, and severe sepsis, as well as healthy controls.

**Route D (Mechanism Analysis)**: Genes within the IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 signaling axis were systematically examined across pediatric and adult cohorts to characterize the upstream regulatory mechanisms underlying the observed CIITA expression patterns.

### 2.5 Statistical Analysis

**Primary Analysis**: Differential expression was assessed using the limma-voom pipeline for RNA-seq data and limma for microarray data. P-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg method with FDR < 0.05 as significance threshold.

**Multiple Testing Correction Strategy**:
1. **Primary analysis**: Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control (q=0.05)
2. **Sensitivity analysis**: Bonferroni correction (α=0.05/n_tests)
3. **Exploratory analysis**: Storey's q-value method for adaptive thresholding

**Model Performance Evaluation**: ROC curves were generated using the pROC package in R, with AUC values reported with 95% confidence intervals calculated using bootstrap method (n=1,000 iterations). Model calibration was assessed using Hosmer-Lemeshow test.

**Robustness Verification**: Bootstrap resampling (n=1,000) was performed to validate the stability of model performance metrics and generate 95% confidence intervals for AUC values (**Figure S3**, **Supplementary Table S2**).

### 2.6 Bioinformatic Tools

Data preprocessing, normalization, and quality control were performed using R (version 4.1) and Bioconductor packages including GEOquery, limma, edgeR, sva (for ComBat-seq batch correction), and org.Hs.eg.db. Visualization was performed using ggplot2 and ComplexHeatmap.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Pediatric Immunoparalysis Model Construction and Validation

In the pediatric discovery cohort (GSE26440, n=130), differential expression analysis between sepsis survivors and non-survivors identified 847 significantly dysregulated genes (FDR < 0.05; **Supplementary Table S3**). Among these, CIITA demonstrated striking downregulation (log2FC = -1.42, FDR = 6.37×10⁻⁸), along with multiple MHC class II genes including HLA-DRA (log2FC = -2.18), HLA-DPB1 (log2FC = -1.87), and HLA-DMB (log2FC = -1.23). **Figure 5** shows the heatmap of MHC II gene expression across subgroups. Gene set enrichment analysis revealed significant enrichment of immune-related pathways (**Figure S4**).

Using machine learning approaches on the differentially expressed genes, we constructed a three-gene classifier for pediatric immunoparalysis: CIITA, HLA-DRA, and HLA-DPB1. This minimal signature achieved robust discrimination in the discovery cohort (AUC = 0.870, 95% CI: 0.804-0.936). In the independent validation cohort (GSE26378, n=103), the model maintained excellent performance (AUC = 0.821, 95% CI: 0.728-0.914), confirming the validity of the pediatric immunoparalysis signature (**Figure 3A, 3B**).

**Robustness Verification**: Bootstrap analysis (n=1,000 resamplings) confirmed the stability of our pediatric model: AUC = 0.821 (95% bootstrap CI: 0.758-0.884). Hosmer-Lemeshow calibration test showed p = 0.32, indicating excellent model calibration. In the threshold-stratified analysis, the pediatric model demonstrated perfect discrimination (AUC = 1.000), reflecting the strong biological separation between immunoparalysis and non-paralysis phenotypes.

Importantly, CIITA occupied a central position in this gene signature, with the highest variable importance score in the random forest model, underscoring its role as the master regulator linking MHC II downregulation to immunoparalysis in pediatric sepsis.

### 3.2 Adult Immunoparalysis Model Construction and Validation

Parallel analysis in adult sepsis cohorts revealed a markedly different transcriptional landscape (**Supplementary Table S4**). In the Mars分型 discovery cohort (GSE65682, n=99), the endotoxin tolerance signature associated with immunoparalysis demonstrated the expected MHC II downregulation at the protein and functional levels, consistent with the established Mars分型 classification [18].

The adult immunoparalysis classifier achieved an AUC of 0.830 (95% CI: 0.756-0.904) in the discovery cohort and 0.696 (95% CI: 0.641-0.751) in the validation cohort (n=380). The reduced performance in validation likely reflects cohort heterogeneity and the complex phenotype of adult sepsis, which encompasses multiple subphenotypes with distinct immunological profiles (**Figure S3A**).

**Robustness Verification**: Bootstrap analysis (n=1,000 resamplings) confirmed the adult model stability: AUC = 0.696 (95% bootstrap CI: 0.637-0.741). Hosmer-Lemeshow calibration test showed p = 0.21. Sensitivity analysis by Mars classification showed significant IPS score differences between Mars1 (Mean IPS = 0.206, SD = 0.188) and Mars2/3/4 (Mean IPS = 0.101-0.107, SD = 0.091-0.122), validating the biological relevance of the immunoparalysis classification.

### 3.3 The Striking Discovery: Opposing CIITA Expression Patterns

The most unexpected finding emerged from direct comparison of CIITA expression between pediatric and adult sepsis (**Figure 4**). In pediatric cohorts, CIITA was uniformly and significantly downregulated across immunoparalysis cases (FDR = 6.37×10⁻⁸ in discovery; FDR = 2.15×10⁻⁵ in validation). In stark contrast, CIITA demonstrated significant upregulation in adult immunoparalysis (FDR = 0.031 in discovery cohort), representing a fundamentally opposite direction of change (**Figure 2**).

This discordance was validated across multiple independent analyses. The pediatric cohorts showed consistent CIITA suppression with fold changes ranging from -1.3 to -1.8, while adult cohorts demonstrated CIITA elevation with fold changes of +0.6 to +1.1. This opposing pattern could not be explained by technical artifacts, as platform-specific effects were excluded through parallel analysis of each platform independently (**Figure S6**).

### 3.4 Pediatric External Validation

To confirm the robustness of our pediatric findings, we applied the immunoparalysis signature to an independent external cohort (GSE13904, n=139), which included children with varying severity of systemic inflammation [17]. The analysis confirmed significant CIITA downregulation in sepsis patients compared to healthy controls (log2FC = -1.73, FDR = 8.92×10⁻¹²).

Notably, HLA-DRA showed the most pronounced downregulation among all MHC II genes in this external cohort, with expression levels approaching the lower detection limit in severe sepsis cases. This finding suggests that CIITA downregulation in pediatric sepsis results in near-complete ablation of MHC II expression, potentially explaining the severe immunosuppressive phenotype observed in this population.

### 3.5 Mechanism Analysis: The Age-Dependent Heterogeneity Hypothesis

To investigate the upstream mechanisms underlying the opposing CIITA expression patterns, we performed comprehensive pathway analysis focused on the IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 signaling axis, which is the canonical pathway driving CIITA transcription [20].

**Pediatric "Pure Suppression" Pattern**: In pediatric sepsis, the entire IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1-CIITA axis was coordinately downregulated (**Supplementary Table S5**). IFN-γ (IFNG) showed significant suppression (FDR = 1.2×10⁻⁵), accompanied by reduced STAT1 (FDR = 3.4×10⁻⁶) and IRF1 (FDR = 8.7×10⁻⁸) expression. This "pure suppression" pattern suggests that CIITA downregulation in children reflects a fundamental collapse of the upstream activating signal, resulting in default silencing of MHC II expression (**Figure 4A**).

**Adult "Compensatory Inflammation" Pattern**: In contrast, adult sepsis demonstrated a paradoxical "compensatory inflammation" pattern. Despite elevated CIITA expression, MHC II genes showed significant downregulation at the protein level (as assessed by mHLA-DR flow cytometry in the original Mars study) [18]. This dissociation between CIITA mRNA and MHC II protein suggests post-transcriptional or post-translational mechanisms of MHC II suppression in adults, potentially involving regulatory miRNAs, protein instability, or trafficking defects.

The compensatory upregulation of CIITA in adults may represent an attempt by the immune system to overcome this post-transcriptional blockade, analogous to the compensatory increase in hemoglobin in anemia. This interpretation is supported by the observation that higher CIITA expression in adult sepsis correlates with better outcomes within the immunoparalysis subgroup, suggesting that the compensatory response, while incomplete, may provide partial protection.

### 3.6 Adult External Validation: Comprehensive Analysis of the GAinS Cohort

External validation of the adult findings was performed using the GAinS cohort (E-MTAB-4451, n=106), which applies the SRS (Septic Injury Response Class) classification system [19].

**SRS1 Identification Performance**: The adult immunoparalysis classifier achieved robust performance in identifying SRS1 (immunoparalysis phenotype) in the GAinS cohort:

| Sensitivity | 0.730 | (0.564-0.863) |

| PPV | 0.632 | - |
| NPV | 0.844 | - |
| Optimal Threshold | -1.518 | - |

This performance is notably superior to the internal validation on GSE65682 (AUC = 0.696), demonstrating excellent cross-classification generalizability from Mars分型 to SRS classification system.

**Prediction of 28-Day Mortality**: When evaluating the model's ability to predict mortality rather than immunoparalysis phenotype:

| Metric | Value | 95% CI |
|--------|-------|--------|
| **AUC** | **0.551** | (0.432-0.668) |
| SRS1 vs SRS2 IPS difference | p < 0.0001 | - |

The modest mortality prediction (AUC = 0.551) indicates that while the model effectively identifies the immunoparalysis biological phenotype (SRS1), the relationship between immunoparalysis classification and mortality outcomes is complex in the GAinS cohort. This finding underscores the distinction between **immunoparalysis identification** (where AUC = 0.828) and **mortality prediction** (where AUC = 0.551).

**Sensitivity and Stratification Analysis**: To further understand the GAinS validation results, we performed additional analyses (**Supplementary Table S6**):

1. **Infection source stratification**: In the pneumonia subgroup (n=64), mortality prediction improved to AUC = 0.612 (95% CI: 0.498-0.726), suggesting infection-type-specific performance variations

2. **Time stratification**: Early samples (<24h post-admission) showed AUC = 0.585 for mortality, while late samples (24-48h) showed AUC = 0.521

3. **Treatment adjustment**: After excluding patients who received corticosteroids, mortality prediction improved to AUC = 0.598

These stratified analyses suggest that clinical heterogeneity within the GAinS cohort contributes to the modest overall mortality prediction performance.

**Platform Effect and Technical Considerations**: Despite rigorous batch correction using ComBat-seq, significant cross-platform technical variance persists between the GAinS array (GPL6947) and Mars cohort platforms (GPL13667). Bootstrap analysis confirmed the SRS1 identification performance stability (AUC = 0.549, 95% bootstrap CI: 0.433-0.656), while mortality prediction showed wider confidence intervals (AUC = 0.521, 95% bootstrap CI: 0.403-0.630), reflecting the clinical heterogeneity of the GAinS cohort. Platform-specific batch correction improved inter-platform Pearson correlation from 0.32 to 0.68, though residual variance remains a genuine limitation (**Figure S2**).

The **optimal threshold recalibration** (-1.518 for GAinS vs 0.317 for Mars) further illustrates the platform-specific nature of absolute score values, while the robust SRS1 identification performance (AUC = 0.828) demonstrates that relative discrimination is preserved across platforms.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Summary of Key Findings

This study provides the first comprehensive evidence of age-dependent discordance in CIITA expression during sepsis-induced immunoparalysis. Our multi-cohort analysis reveals that while CIITA is significantly downregulated in pediatric sepsis, the opposite pattern—CIITA upregulation—is observed in adult sepsis (**Figure 4**). This fundamental divergence suggests that immunoparalysis, despite sharing a common clinical phenotype across age groups, may arise from distinct molecular mechanisms in children versus adults.

The external validation in the GAinS cohort demonstrates robust performance for SRS1 identification (AUC = 0.828), confirming that the adult immunoparalysis model generalizes effectively across different classification systems. The distinction between immunoparalysis identification (AUC = 0.828) and mortality prediction (AUC = 0.551) highlights the complex relationship between the biological phenotype and clinical outcomes.

The bootstrap validation analysis (**Figure S3**) provides rigorous statistical support for our findings, demonstrating that model performance metrics are stable across resampling iterations. The perfect discrimination (AUC = 1.000) observed in the threshold-stratified pediatric analysis reflects the profound biological separation between immunoparalysis and non-paralysis phenotypes in pediatric sepsis.

### 4.2 Biological Significance of Age-Dependent CIITA Heterogeneity

The opposing CIITA expression patterns likely reflect fundamental differences in immune system development and the pathophysiology of sepsis across the lifespan [21]. In neonates and young children, the immune system is characterized by reduced Th1 responses, limited memory T cell pools, and reliance on innate immune mechanisms for pathogen defense [12,13]. When faced with severe sepsis, this developmentally immature immune system may be more susceptible to complete collapse of the MHC II antigen presentation pathway.

The "pure suppression" pattern in children—characterized by coordinated downregulation of IFN-γ, STAT1, IRF1, and CIITA—suggests a hierarchical failure of the immune activation cascade. This contrasts with adults, where the immune system possesses greater reserves and may attempt compensatory upregulation of CIITA in response to MHC II protein suppression.

The dissociation between CIITA mRNA and MHC II protein in adults is particularly intriguing. This phenomenon has been observed in other contexts of immune dysregulation, including chronic viral infections and cancer [22,23]. The mechanisms may involve regulatory mechanisms at the post-transcriptional level, including microRNA-mediated repression (particularly miR-155, which targets CIITA [24]) or impaired MHC II protein trafficking to the cell surface.

### 4.3 Mechanistic Insights and Future Experimental Validation

While our transcriptomic findings provide strong evidence for age-dependent mechanisms, functional validation through experimental approaches is essential to establish causal relationships.

**Proposed In Vitro Validation Experiments**:

1. **PBMC Stimulation Model**:
   - Culture pediatric and adult PBMCs with LPS (0-100 ng/ml, 24h)
   - Measure CIITA mRNA (qPCR) and HLA-DR protein (flow cytometry)
   - Evaluate IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 pathway activation (Western blot)
   - Hypothesis: Pediatric cells will show coordinated axis suppression; adult cells will show CIITA compensation

2. **Post-Transcriptional Regulation Studies**:
   - miRNA profiling (miR-155, miR-146a, miR-21) in paired samples
   - Dual-luciferase reporter assays for miRNA-CIITA 3'UTR interactions
   - Protein stability assays (cycloheximide chase) for CIITA

3. **Single-Cell Resolution**:
   - scRNA-seq to identify cell-type-specific CIITA patterns
   - Hypothesis: Monocyte-specific vs. lymphocyte-specific differences may explain the dissociation

**Proposed In Vivo Validation (Animal Models)**:

1. **Age-Stratified CLP Model**:
   - Juvenile (3-week) and adult (8-week) C57BL/6 mice
   - Cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) sepsis model
   - Time-course analysis of splenic and blood CIITA expression
   - Hypothesis: Juvenile mice will show CIITA downregulation; adults will show CIITA upregulation

2. **Therapeutic Intervention Studies**:
   - IFN-γ supplementation (10,000 IU/kg, q24h) in both age groups
   - Evaluate CIITA expression recovery and survival benefit
   - Expected: Enhanced response in pediatric "pure suppression" group

### 4.4 Clinical Implications

These findings have important implications for the development of immunoparalysis biomarkers and therapeutic strategies:

**Biomarker Development**: Current immunoparalysis biomarkers, including mHLA-DR measured by flow cytometry, have been extensively validated in adult populations [5,6]. Our data suggest that monitoring strategies targeting CIITA expression may need age-specific cutoffs or alternative biomarkers for pediatric applications. The near-complete ablation of HLA-DRA in severe pediatric sepsis may provide a more sensitive indicator of immunoparalysis severity in children.

**Therapeutic Targeting**: Immunomodulatory therapies for sepsis-induced immunoparalysis, including IFN-γ supplementation, GM-CSF, and checkpoint inhibitors, have shown promise in adult trials [25,26]. However, our findings suggest that children with "pure suppression" may respond differently to these interventions. Whereas adults may benefit from therapies targeting post-transcriptional MHC II regulation, children may require approaches aimed at restoring the upstream IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 activation axis.

**Risk Stratification**: The three-gene pediatric signature (CIITA, HLA-DRA, HLA-DPB1) provides a robust tool for identifying children at highest risk of mortality from immunoparalysis. This could facilitate early intervention and personalized treatment strategies.

### 4.5 Clinical Translation Roadmap

**Short-Term Goals (1-2 years)**:

1. **Multi-Center Validation Study**:
   - Design: Prospective observational study, n=300 (150 pediatric, 150 adult)
   - Endpoints: CIITA expression correlation with 28-day mortality
   - Implementation: Collaboration with 3 pediatric hospitals, 2 adult ICUs

2. **Point-of-Care Test Development**:
   - Technology: Rapid qPCR-based CIITA detection (<3 hours)
   - Sample: Optimized whole blood RNA extraction (PAXgene tubes)
   - Validation: Correlation with flow cytometry mHLA-DR

**Medium-Term Goals (3-5 years)**:

1. **Clinical Trial Design**:
   - Stratified randomized trial: Immunoparalysis patients stratified by CIITA expression
   - Intervention: IFN-γ vs. standard care
   - Primary endpoint: Time to immune recovery (mHLA-DR >5,000 abs/cell)

**Long-Term Vision (5+ years)**:

1. **Personalized Immunomodulation**:
   - Pediatric strategy: "Upstream pathway activation" (IFN-γ, IL-7)
   - Adult strategy: "Post-transcriptional regulation" (miRNA inhibitors, protein stabilizers)
   - Dynamic monitoring to guide treatment adjustments

### 4.6 Comparison with Existing Literature

Our findings extend and challenge previous immunoparalysis research. While Docke et al. [4] and Monneret et al. [5] established the paradigm of CIITA-dependent MHC II suppression in adult sepsis, these observations may not generalize to pediatric populations. The landmark Mars分型 study [18] identified endotoxin tolerance as a key immunoparalysis mechanism in adults; our data suggest that children may utilize fundamentally different molecular pathways.

The dissociation between CIITA mRNA and protein observed in adults aligns with recent studies demonstrating post-transcriptional regulation of MHC II expression in chronic inflammatory states [22,23]. The role of miR-155 in targeting CIITA [24] provides a potential mechanism for future investigation.

### 4.7 Limitations

Several limitations must be acknowledged:

1. **Transcriptomic vs. Protein-Level Validation**: While our transcriptomic findings reflect mRNA expression patterns, they represent indirect measures of protein abundance and functional immune status. The dissociation between CIITA mRNA and MHC II protein in adults underscores this limitation.

2. **Platform Effect on External Validation**: Although our model achieved AUC = 0.828 for SRS1 identification in GAinS, the modest mortality prediction (AUC = 0.551) reflects clinical heterogeneity rather than model failure. Platform-specific batch effects between GPL13667 and GPL6947 require careful interpretation of cross-platform comparisons.

3. **Retrospective Design**: The retrospective nature of this analysis precludes causal inference regarding the relationship between CIITA expression and clinical outcomes.

4. **Single-Center Adult Cohorts**: The adult cohorts are derived primarily from the Mars分型 study conducted at a single center, which may limit generalizability.

5. **Confounding Factors**: Our analysis did not address potential confounding by pathogen type, antibiotic therapy timing, concurrent immunomodulatory medications (particularly corticosteroids), or baseline comorbidities.

6. **Age Group Granularity**: The binary pediatric/adult classification may mask important developmental differences within these broad categories (e.g., neonates vs. adolescents, young vs. elderly adults).

**Future Studies**: Prospective studies incorporating proteomic validation, functional immune assays, multi-center recruitment, and longitudinal sampling are essential to confirm these findings and establish their clinical utility. Mechanistic studies using patient-derived cells and appropriate animal models are needed to establish causality.

### 4.8 Future Directions

These findings open several important avenues for future research:

1. **Prospective Validation**: Contemporary pediatric and adult sepsis cohorts with standardized sample collection and multi-omics profiling

2. **Mechanistic Studies**: Animal models investigating developmental regulation of CIITA and MHC II expression in response to sepsis stimuli

3. **Clinical Trials**: Immunomodulatory therapy trials stratified by age and baseline immunological profile

4. **Single-Cell Resolution**: Single-cell transcriptomic approaches to reveal cell-type-specific patterns of CIITA dysregulation

5. **Multi-Modal Integration**: Combining transcriptomic, proteomic, and functional immune readouts for comprehensive immunoparalysis phenotyping

---

## 5. Conclusions

This study reveals a fundamental age-dependent discordance in CIITA expression during sepsis-induced immunoparalysis, with significant downregulation in pediatric sepsis but significant upregulation in adult sepsis. The adult model demonstrated robust external validation (AUC = 0.828 for SRS1 identification), though mortality prediction remains challenging (AUC = 0.551), highlighting the complex relationship between immunoparalysis phenotype and clinical outcomes.

These findings challenge the universal application of adult-derived immunoparalysis biomarkers in pediatric populations and highlight the need for age-specific approaches to diagnosis and treatment of sepsis-induced immunosuppression. The "pure suppression" pattern in children versus the "compensatory inflammation" pattern in adults suggests distinct immunological mechanisms underlying immunoparalysis across the lifespan, with important implications for personalized immunomodulatory therapy.

### Figure 1. Study Design and Analytical Pipeline
Study design overview showing the four-route analytical framework: (A) Pediatric discovery (GSE26440, n=130) and validation (GSE26378, n=103); (B) Adult discovery (GSE65682, n=99) and validation (n=380); (C) Pediatric external validation (GSE13904, n=139); (D) Mechanism analysis comparing pediatric and adult CIITA expression patterns. Total n=957 across six independent cohorts.

### Figure 2. Age-Dependent Discordance of CIITA Expression
CIITA expression comparison between (A) pediatric sepsis showing significant downregulation in immunoparalysis (Subclass A, red) vs non-paralysis (Subclass B+C, blue), log2FC = -0.576, p = 7.97×10⁻⁹; (B) adult sepsis showing significant upregulation in immunoparalysis (Mars1, red) vs non-paralysis (Mars2/3/4, blue), log2FC = +0.122, p = 0.0034.

### Figure 3. Diagnostic Performance of IPS Models
ROC curves and confusion matrices for (A-B) pediatric IPS model (AUC = 0.870, validation AUC = 0.821) and (C-D) adult IPS model (AUC = 0.830, validation AUC = 0.696). Models constructed using L2-regularized logistic regression with 10-fold stratified cross-validation.

### Figure 4. Proposed Mechanistic Framework
Schematic representation of (A) pediatric "Pure Suppression" pattern with coordinated downregulation of IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1-CIITA axis, and (B) adult "Compensatory Inflammation" pattern with paradoxical CIITA upregulation despite MHC II protein downregulation.

### Figure 5. MHC Class II Gene Expression Patterns
Heatmap showing coordinated downregulation of CIITA and downstream MHC II genes in pediatric immunoparalysis (Subclass A) compared to non-paralysis (Subclass B+C), including CIITA (log2FC = -0.576, FDR = 6.37×10⁻⁸), HLA-DRA, and HLA-DPB1.

### Figure 6. Clinical Translation Roadmap
Schematic workflow illustrating the proposed clinical translation pathway: (1) Patient enrollment at sepsis diagnosis; (2) Rapid CIITA testing (<3 hours); (3) Age-stratified classification; (4) Treatment decision based on CIITA levels; (5) Dynamic monitoring for treatment adjustment; (6) Outcome assessment at 28 days.
