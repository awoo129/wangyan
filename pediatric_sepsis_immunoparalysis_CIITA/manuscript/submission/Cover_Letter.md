## Summary of Key Findings

Our multi-cohort transcriptomic analysis (n=957 across six independent datasets) reveals, for the first time, a fundamental **age-dependent discordance in CIITA expression** during sepsis-induced immunoparalysis:

- **In pediatric sepsis**: CIITA is significantly downregulated (FDR = 6.37×10⁻⁸), with coordinated suppression of the entire IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1-CIITA axis ("pure suppression" pattern)
- **In adult sepsis**: CIITA demonstrates significant upregulation (FDR = 0.031) despite MHC II protein downregulation ("compensatory inflammation" pattern)

A three-gene pediatric signature (CIITA, HLA-DRA, HLA-DPB1) achieved robust diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.870 in discovery, AUC = 0.821 in validation).

## Clinical Significance

These findings carry substantial clinical implications:

1. **Biomarker Development**: Current immunoparalysis biomarkers have been exclusively validated in adults. Our data challenge the universal application of these biomarkers in pediatric populations, necessitating age-specific approaches.

2. **Therapeutic Targeting**: Children with "pure suppression" may require fundamentally different immunomodulatory strategies than adults, with focus on restoring the upstream IFN-γ-STAT1-IRF1 activation axis rather than post-transcriptional MHC II regulation.

3. **Risk Stratification**: The validated three-gene signature provides a clinically applicable tool for identifying pediatric patients at highest risk of mortality from immunoparalysis.

## Novelty and Impact

This study represents a paradigm shift in our understanding of sepsis-induced immunoparalysis by demonstrating that a cornerstone mechanism (CIITA dysregulation) operates in opposite directions across age groups. This age-dependent heterogeneity has been overlooked due to the historical focus on adult-only cohorts in immunoparalysis research.
